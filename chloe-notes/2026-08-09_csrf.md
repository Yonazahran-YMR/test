# CSRF Notes (Cross-Site Request Forgery)

Okay, so ngl this confused me at first, but it finally clicked. 

## The Core Concept
CSRF is basically when a malicious site hijacks your browser's blind trust. If you're logged into a site (like your bank), your browser automatically sends your session cookies with every request. An attacker takes advantage of that and tricks your browser into doing something you never agreed to. 

## How it Actually Works
* You log into a legit site (say, yourbank.com).
* You wander off to some sketchy corner of the internet, like `totally-legit-cat-videos.com`.
* That sketchy site has hidden code that secretly fires off a request to your bank: *"Hey, transfer 500 bucks to this guy."*
* Because your active session cookie tags along automatically, the bank thinks *you* meant to do it. Boom. Hack achieved.

## How We Stop It
Thankfully, we aren't completely defenseless against this. 
* **Anti-CSRF Tokens:** The server hands your browser a random, secret string when you load a page. When you submit a form, you gotta hand that token back. The malicious third-party site has no clue what the token is, so their forged request gets blocked. 
* **SameSite Cookie Attributes:** This is basically telling the browser, "Hey, stop blindly attaching my cookies when requests come from a different website."
